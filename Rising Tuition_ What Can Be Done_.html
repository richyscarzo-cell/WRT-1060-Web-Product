The Rising Cost of College Tuition

Students, families, and administrators all feel the squeeze. Here is how we got here and what can be done to change it.

The Problem

  • Tuition has outpaced inflation for decades.
  • Many prospective students fear lifelong debt.
  • Enrolled students face tight budgets and heavy loans.
  • Families must plan years in advance.

Why Is College So Expensive?

No single cause explains it. The research points to several overlapping forces.

SourceKey finding
Berger & Kostal (2002)Less government funding links to higher tuition.
Combs (2026)State subsidy changes shift how colleges spend.
Hemelt & Marcotte (2016)Big tuition hikes push students toward two-year colleges.
Scogin (2025)A college president argues rising expenses drive tuition.
Hanson (2025)Aid, hidden prices, and limited competition may all play a role.
Nova (2022)Explains the long history behind today’s prices.

Possible Solutions

1. Restore state funding

  • Pro: Directly offsets tuition
  • Con: Costs taxpayers
  • Evidence: Berger & Kostal; Combs

2. Clearer pricing

  • Pro: Families compare true costs
  • Con: Colleges may resist
  • Evidence: Hanson

3. Rein in college costs

  • Pro: Targets the spending side
  • Con: May affect services
  • Evidence: Scogin; Combs

4. Two-year pathways

  • Pro: Lower entry price
  • Con: Transfer hurdles
  • Evidence: Hemelt & Marcotte

Some experts argue higher spending improves education quality, while others say prices rose too quickly. Weigh both views.

What Can You Do?

Students

  • File the FAFSA every year.
  • Borrow only what you need.

Families

  • Compare net price, not sticker price.
  • Explore federal aid options.

Administrators

  • Publish clear, true costs.
  • Review spending priorities openly.
